# Second-order slope geometry via collision pairs

Experimental round, **no Lean formalisation, no new axioms, no `sorry`** (nothing in the Lean
sources was touched).  Script: `analysis/second_order_slope_geometry.py`; raw log:
`analysis/second_order_slope_geometry_output.txt`; per-family Phase-1 data is
persisted to `analysis/second_order_phase1/*.jsonl` (8.6 MB, regenerated by the script on demand
and therefore not committed).  All arithmetic is exact over prime fields (Python
integers mod `p`, `fractions.Fraction` for the two normalised invariants).  No floats enter any
verdict.

## Question

`LIST_GEOMETRY_FRONTIER.md` established
`A − 1 ≤ #Bad ≤ (e+1)·A + 1` and showed that the *first-order* invariants
(`|L|`, `dim W`, `max|Z(p)|`, syndrome dimension, pairwise zero sets) do not distinguish
instances with different `#Bad`.  This round asks the sharper question:

> does any **second-order** invariant — of the configuration of witness supports of the
> realised slopes — carry information beyond the first-order invariants?

Method: **collision pairs**.  Find two gap instances whose entire first-order vector agrees
exactly while `#Bad` differs, then ask which second-order invariants differ on them.

## Setup and conventions

Gap instances only: `w = d(f₁,C) ≠ 0`, `¬(2e < w)`, `¬(2e + 2w < d)`.
Instances are enumerated over coset representatives of `Fⁿ/C` (the bad set depends on `f₁` only
through its coset, a proved Lean fact), exhaustively in the small families and over a fixed
deterministic subsample of representatives in the larger ones.

*Canonical witness.*  A bad parameter may admit several witness codewords; the lexicographically
least one is used, and the slope of `γ` is taken relative to the smallest bad parameter `γ₀`.
For the two explicit witness pairs quoted in §4 the witness is **unique** for every bad
parameter of the RS[6,2]/F₇ pair, so the negative conclusion there does not depend on that
convention.

*Three support families* (the mission's `W_γ` is ambiguous between "bad parameter" and
"realised slope"; all three natural readings were computed):

| tag | index set | support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SD` | realised slopes `q` (size `A`) | `W_q = {i : f₁(i) ≠ q(i)}` — the positions the charging argument spends on the class of `q` |
| `SU` | realised slopes `q` (size `A`) | positions of `W_q` actually used by some `γ` of the class |
| `PE` | bad parameters `γ` (size `#Bad`) | error support `{i : (f₀+γf₁)(i) ≠ c_γ(i)}` of `γ`'s canonical witness |

`SD`/`SU` are the *slope-configuration* object the mission asks about.  `PE` is auxiliary and,
crucially, **its index set is the target itself** (`|PE| = #Bad`), so `PE`-invariants cannot
bound `#Bad`; they are reported separately and their family size `PE_m` (and likewise
`SD_m = SU_m = A`) is excluded from every verdict as tautological.

*Second-order vector* per family: `E₂, E_max, E_min`, intersection histogram `H`; degree vector
statistics `D₁, D₂, D₃, d_max, N_j`, plus the exact rationals `D₁/m` and the degree variance;
incidence-matrix ranks over `F_p` and over `F₂`; Gram matrix `G = MMᵀ` (rank over `F₂`, number of
distinct rows, maximal off-diagonal entry, number of zero off-diagonal entries); pattern
multiplicity `P, m_max`; slope-interaction graph (components, largest component, edges,
triangles); and — added as the *maximal possible* second-order invariant — the full
**isomorphism type** `_iso` of the support hypergraph, i.e. the canonical form of the whole
configuration under relabelling of the `n` positions.

## Phase 1 — baseline

**38 084** gap instances over RS[4,2]/F₅, RS[4,3]/F₅, RS[5,2]/F₅, RS[5,3]/F₅, RS[6,2]/F₇,
RS[6,3]/F₇, RS[6,4]/F₇, RS[7,3]/F₇, RS[5,2]/F₁₁, RS[6,3]/F₁₁, RS[6,2]/F₁₃, radii `e ∈ {1,2}`;
**18 473** of them have `#Bad ≥ 2` and `A ≥ 2`.  Extremes: `max #Bad = 11`, `max A = 9`,
`max |L| = 136`.  Progress is persisted per `(family, e)`.

## Phase 2 — collision pairs

Matching key `(p, n, k, d, e, w, |L|, dim W, maxZeroW, dim Synd)`, at most two representatives
per `#Bad` value per signature:

| regime | matching key | pairs | signatures |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I `A` free | first-order vector | **256** | 21 |
| II `A` pinned | first-order vector **and** `A` | **291** | 42 |
| III `A` + list-distance multiset pinned | + multiset `{d(f₁,q) : q ∈ L}` | **291** | 42 |

Regime II is the decisive one: since `A` and `#Bad` determine each other up to the factor
`e + 1`, an invariant that merely re-reads `A` would "separate" in regime I for trivial reasons.
`#Bad`-gaps occurring in the collision pairs range over `(3,4), (4,5), …, (7,10)`.

## Phases 3–4 — discrimination

### Slope-indexed families `SD`, `SU` (the mission object)

* **No invariant separates all collision pairs — in any regime.**  Best performers,
  regime I: `SU_iso` 252/256, `SU_D₂ = SU_D₃` 246/256, `SU_dvar` 245/256, `SD_iso` 242/256,
  `SD_D₂` 241/256.  Regime II: `SU_iso` 265/291, `SU_Hist` 253/291, `SU_D₃` 252/291,
  `SD_iso` 212/291, `SD_E₂` 188/291.
* **No invariant is monotone in `#Bad`.**  Every separator that admits an order moves in both
  directions, e.g. regime II `SU_E₂`: 188 up / 41 down; `SD_D₁`: 122 up / 55 down;
  `SU_Grank₂`: 103 up / 60 down.  There is therefore no candidate for an inequality whose
  left-hand side grows with `A` or `#Bad`.
* **Invariants that separate no pair at all.**  Regime I: `SD_m_max, SD_ncomp, SU_m_max,
  SU_ncomp`.  Regime II additionally: `SD_P, SD_Gdistinct, SD_maxcomp, SU_P, SU_Gdistinct,
  SU_maxcomp` — i.e. pattern multiplicity, Gram-row multiplicity and connectivity of the slope
  interaction graph are completely blind.
* **Unresolved pairs (the hard core).**  The *entire* slope-indexed sub-vector, isomorphism type
  included, is constant on **4 / 256** pairs in regime I and on **26 / 291** pairs in
  regime II (identically in regime III).

### Parameter-indexed family `PE` (auxiliary)

`PE_D₂, PE_D₃, PE_Gdistinct, PE_Hist, PE_Ndeg, PE_P, PE_iso` (and `PE_D₁` in regime II)
separate **all** pairs, and `PE_D₁, PE_D₂, PE_Gdistinct, PE_P` do so monotonically in `#Bad`.
This is expected and carries no information: the family is indexed by the bad set itself
(`|PE| = #Bad`), so these are statistics *of* the object to be bounded, not constraints *on* it.
`PE_E_min` and `PE_m_max` separate nothing even so.

## §4 Explicit witness pairs

Regime I (witness unique for every bad parameter in both instances, so witness-choice
independent), RS[6,2]/F₇ on `D = {1,…,6}`, `k = 2`, `e = 2`, `w = 3`, `|L| = 9`, `A = 2`:

```
X₁ : f₀ = (0,0,5,3,3,6), f₁ = (0,0,1,6,2,4)   #Bad = 3
     slopes (5,3,1,6,4,2) with W = {0,1,4,5}, class {0,1}
            (6,0,1,2,3,4) with W = {0,3,4},   class {0,2}
X₂ : f₀ = (0,0,0,4,1,0), f₁ = (0,0,1,6,2,4)   #Bad = 4
     slopes (0,5,3,1,6,4) with W = {1,2,3,4}, class {0,2}
            (6,0,1,2,3,4) with W = {0,3,4},   class {0,1,6}
```

Both give the **same** complete slope-indexed vector, including
`iso = ((0,1,2),(0,1,3,4))`, `E₂ = 2`, `Ndeg = ((0,1),(1,3),(2,2))`, `D₁,D₂,D₃ = 7,11,19`,
`rank_p = rank₂ = 2`, `Grank₂ = 1`, `edges = 1`, `tri = 0`.

Regime II, RS[5,2]/F₅ on `D = {0,…,4}`, `e = 2`, `w = 1`, `|L| = 18`, `A = 2`:

```
X₁ : f₀ = (0,0,0,1,1), f₁ = (0,0,0,0,1)   #Bad = 4, class sizes (3,2)
X₂ : f₀ = (0,0,1,1,3), f₁ = (0,0,0,0,1)   #Bad = 5, class sizes (3,3)
```

again with identical slope-indexed vector, `iso = ((0,1,2),(0,1,3))`.

**Mechanism.**  In every unresolved pair the only quantity that differs is the **occupancy**
multiset `{|class(q)|}` of the slope classes; with all classes containing the reference
parameter one has the identity `Σ_q |class(q)| = #Bad + A − 1`.  The support geometry of the
slope family — how the classes sit in the position set, how they intersect, what their incidence
ranks and hypergraph isomorphism type are — is *identical* while the occupancies differ.  Since
occupancy is exactly what the already-proved packing bound `|class(q)| ≤ cap(e, d(f₁,q))`
controls (`Alphabet.card_le_chargeCap`), the residual information sits where the existing theorem
already sits, not in the interaction geometry.

## Appendix — pairwise-budget probe (all 38 084 gap instances)

| candidate | violations |
| --- | --- |
| `A ≤ e + 1` | 5 833 |
| `A ≤ 2e` | 2 472 |
| `A ≤ w + 1` | 6 359 |
| `A ≤ n` | 103 |
| `A(A−1) ≤ n(n−1)` | 103 |
| `A(A−1) ≤ 2e·n` | 739 |
| `A(A−1) ≤ 2e·w` | 6 519 |
| `A(A−1) ≤ |L|` | 1 063 |
| `A ≤ |L|` | **0** (already known: every realised slope is a secant slope, hence lies in `L`) |
| `A(A−1) ≤ (e+1)·|L|` | **0** (unrefuted on these families, unproved, and slack — e.g. `A(A−1)=72` vs `(e+1)|L| = 384`) |

Passing from `A` to the pairwise budget `A(A−1)` therefore buys nothing here: every candidate
right-hand side that fails for `A` fails for `A(A−1)` on the same worst instance
(RS[6,3]/F₁₁, `e = 2`, `w = 2`, `|L| = 128`, `A = 9`, `#Bad = 10`), and the only surviving
inequalities are the ones already implied by `A ≤ |L|`.

## Success criterion — the three answers

1. **Do second-order invariants separate collision pairs?**  Partially, never completely.  Of
   the slope-indexed invariants the best (`SU_iso`, the full isomorphism type of the used-witness
   configuration) separates 252/256 pairs in regime I and 265/291 in regime II; 4 and 26 pairs
   respectively survive the entire second-order slope vector.  The parameter-indexed family
   separates everything, but tautologically — it is indexed by the bad set itself.
2. **Weakest separator?**  Not applicable: no invariant separates all pairs, so there is no
   "weakest separator" to report.  The strongest partial separators are `SU_iso`, then
   `SU_D₃/SU_Ndeg/SU_Hist`, then `SU_dvar` and `SU_E₂`; the intersection *energies* `E₂`
   specifically asked about in the mission separate at most 229/291 pairs and are non-monotone
   (188 up / 41 down), so they support no inequality.
3. **Is the geometric-interaction route closed?**  On the families tested, yes, with explicit
   evidence: there exist gap instances agreeing in all first-order invariants **and** in `A`
   **and** in the list-distance multiset, whose slope-support configurations are *isomorphic as
   hypergraphs*, but whose `#Bad` differ (§4).  No invariant of the slope-support configuration
   — energies, degrees, incidence/Gram ranks, interaction graph, or even the full isomorphism
   type — can distinguish them, because the difference lives entirely in the per-class
   occupancy.  Any bound going beyond `#Bad ≤ (e+1)·A + 1` must therefore use data outside the
   slope-support configuration.

## What is *not* claimed

* No theorem.  These are exact computations over the listed finite families, not general proofs;
  the negative statements are exact counterexamples on the instances recorded, and the positive
  ones (`A ≤ |L|` aside, which is already proved) are unrefuted conjectures on those families
  only.
* No claim about larger `n`, larger fields, `e ≥ 3`, or the M31 parameters.
* Nothing here bounds `|L(f₁,2e)|`, and no Johnson/capacity statement was used or produced.
